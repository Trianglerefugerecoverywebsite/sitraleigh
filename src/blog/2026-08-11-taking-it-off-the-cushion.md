Meditation is a wonderful practice and for many, equated with Buddhism. It is true that the Buddha said meditation is the practice that leads to a direct realization  or awakening (in the Satipatthana). The Tibetan Buddhist practice of Lamrim, which is a year-long meditation practice, says something very similar regarding the results from dedicated and focused meditation. 



Meditation has physical, mental and emotional benefits. We can become calmer and more relaxed. Through the practice of noticing thoughts arise and pass we can learn to not get caught up in repetitive thinking. Compassion, kindness and joy can also be a result of sustained practice.



Thinking about my own relationship to the cushion, I find it hard to imagine life without "sitting" and for better or worse, being a meditator is certainly part of my identity.



If we consider our time on the cushion however, what is it really for? Is it so we have perfect posture for the entire sit? A clear and stable mind, devoid of internal distractions or "monkey mind"?  Sitting every day without ever missing? The list of how we might imagine a perfect practice is individual, yet not the point. 



In my opinion, we do not practice meditation to become better at meditation, although that happens. Our skills do increase. The ability to be in stillness, to allow the mind to settle as well as the length of time we can sit increases. The real reason however is to become better at life. Meditation allows us an opportunity to get to know our own minds. We learn to relate to the present moment in a different way. We see the three marks of existence, annica, annatta and dukkha with more clarity.(impermanence, not self and dissatisfaction).  Mindfulness becomes something we embody slowly, but this awareness changes how we relate and respond to the world. 

The Noble Eightfold path also becomes something we begin to embody.



Just think how different life becomes when we speak and act from a place of compassion and non-harming.
