These Eight factors,  understanding or view, intention or resolve, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and collectedness are the tools he offered to us so we could wake up and stop suffering. The Buddha didn't make promises and then expect us to decipher some paradoxical statements to figure out the secret to awakening. This doesn't mean he never said anything that wasn't hard to understand, he did - But the core Teachings, the one's that I believe are the most important for us to engage with he gave as clear as day.  Take these Eight factors, get to know them, put them into action in your life and things will change.   



This week's teaching we take a look at the first four, what they mean, how they work together and what we can expect when we put them into action. These four include the two Wisdom (Panna) factors and two of the three Ethical Factors, (Sila). My view on how to use them begins with comprehension of their meaning from a Buddhist perspective and in this context. Once we basically know what they mean, we can begin awareness of them in our day to day life and put them into action.  One good tip is to write the eight of them down, or better yet create a  Buddhist teachings page to carry with you, in your own handwriting, write down the eight factors and glance at it once or twice a day. You will shortly memorize them and have them handy in the mind.  It can be very helpful.
